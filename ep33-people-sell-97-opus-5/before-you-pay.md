# Before you pay for an AI video editing course

## Five questions

1. **What exactly do I get?** Videos, a tool, a community, updates? Write it down. A tool you can only reach while
   you pay a monthly fee stops working for you the day you stop paying.
2. **Is the tool itself free somewhere?** Search GitHub for "claude video editor" or "claude code video editing" and
   sort by stars. Read the license file: MIT means you may use, change and share it.
3. **What will I still pay every month?** A Claude plan (Pro is $20 a month billed monthly; Opus is not on the
   Free plan), plus any paid voice, image or video services the setup calls.
4. **Can I try it on one of my own videos this week?** Pick one raw clip and give it 30 minutes with a free editor
   first. If you get stuck on something specific, that is the question a course should answer.
5. **Who is selling it, and do they show their own results?** Look for their own videos made this way, with dates.

## Three free editors to try first (checked Oct 27, 2026)

| editor | what it says it does | license |
|---|---|---|
| [riecodes/free247editor](https://github.com/riecodes/free247editor) | "Free, open-source personal AI video editor for Claude. Drop in a raw video, say "edit this", give notes in plain words." | MIT |
| [browser-use/video-use](https://github.com/browser-use/video-use) | "Edit videos with coding agents" | MIT |
| [kevinbadi/open-edits](https://github.com/kevinbadi/open-edits) | "Claude Code skills that turn a raw talking-head clip into a fully animated short." | MIT |

These are other people's projects. Read each README before you run anything, and check what it installs and which
paid services it may call.
