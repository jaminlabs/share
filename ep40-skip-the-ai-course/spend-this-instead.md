# Before you pay for an AI course

This is my opinion, from building with AI every day: most AI courses teach a workflow that someone open-sources for
free a few days later. The money does more in a subscription you use every day.

## 1. Check the course in five minutes
1. Write down the one thing the course promises you will be able to do ("automate my inbox", "edit shorts with AI").
2. Search GitHub for that thing plus "open source", and read the tool's own docs. If a free repo or the official guide
   already does it, the course is mostly a walkthrough of that.
3. Ask: will this still be true in three months? Prompt tricks and tool tours usually aren't. Fundamentals (how the
   models fail, how to check their work, how to give them your own data) usually are.

## 2. Where to put the money instead
- One AI subscription you will open every day (the chat app plus its coding agent, if it has one). One you use beats
  three you forget.
- A small budget for the tools your system needs (a domain, a cheap server, an API key with a spending cap).
- Your time, spent on one project that only you can make, because it runs on your work, your data or your taste.
  That is the part nobody can open-source for you.

## 3. Let the subscription teach you by building
Paste this into your AI app and fill in the brackets:

```
I want to be able to [the one thing the course promised].
My situation: [what I do every day, which tools I already use, how much time I have per day].
Don't explain the theory first. Build the smallest working version with me, one step at a time.
After each step, tell me what to check so I know it works, and wait for my answer.
When it works, list what would make my version hard for someone else to copy.
```

## 4. Make it hard to copy
Easy apps and easy tutorials get copied in a day or two. What lasts: something that keeps getting better because you
use it, that fits one kind of person very well, and that people trust because they watched you build it.
