# The 4-hour edit audit (from "Four hours per video")

Before I automated my editing, one video took me about four hours to edit, plus an hour or two to shoot. In the
Short I list what those hours went to. This folder turns that list into a checklist you can run on your own next
edit: time every step, then mark which ones an AI agent could take off your plate today.

## How to use it

1. Open `four-hour-audit.md` next to your editor.
2. On your next video, write down how many minutes each step really took. A phone stopwatch is fine.
3. Mark each step Y, P or N: can an agent do it from your files today (Y), part of it (P), or not at all (N)?
   The last column has one thing to try per step. None of them needs an editor app.
4. Start with the biggest Y. Don't automate a step you haven't timed.

## What I found when I did this

- Shooting stays yours. An agent can't film you, but it can hand you the lines to say before you press record.
- Retakes don't go away. An agent can read your transcript and tell you what's missing or unclear, but you still
  film the retake.
- The first steps I handed over were the mechanical ones: cutting pauses and repeats, putting music under the
  voice and ducking it, and checking every number on screen against what I actually said.
- Taste stays with you: which sound goes where, and whether the video is good enough to post.

The four hours are my own estimate from editing by hand, not a study. Your numbers will differ, and that's the
point of timing them.
