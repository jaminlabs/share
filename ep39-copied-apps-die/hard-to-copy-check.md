# Could a weekend clone replace it? A copy test for AI products

Answer each question with yes or no for the product you're building (or paying for). Be strict: "we plan to" counts
as no.

## Does it keep getting better?

1. Has it shipped a change users can notice in the last two weeks?
2. Does it get better for each user the more they use it (their corrections, their files, their style)?
3. If you stopped working on it for a month, would users notice?

## Does it understand the person using it?

4. Does it remember what this user wants (their design intent, their words, their past choices) without being told
   again?
5. Would a brand-new copy, with the same model and the same prompts, give this user worse results on day one?

## Does it take real time to build?

6. Did it take more than a few days of work that a stranger can't see from the outside (data, testing, edge cases)?
7. Is there something in it you collected or tuned yourself, not just a model call and a nice screen?

## Would people trust you to keep it running?

8. Do the people who use it know who you are (your name, your face, your audience)?
9. Have you fixed a user's problem within a day before, and could they say so?
10. Would a buyer believe you'll still be maintaining it next year?

## Count the yeses

- **0-3:** a weekend clone can replace it. Pick the one area above where you can get a yes fastest.
- **4-6:** a copy would hurt but not kill it. The questions you answered no to are where a copy catches up.
- **7-10:** hard to copy. Keep the update rhythm: the day it stalls, the clock starts for a copy.

A Photoshop-style editor can get away with a low score because its core barely moves. An AI app can't: users expect
it to understand them better every week.
